Frietjes

The crispy, golden pride of Belgium

📜 Origin

Frietjes are deep-fried potato sticks claimed proudly by Belgium. Stories trace them to the Meuse Valley, where villagers fried potatoes when rivers froze and fish were scarce.

Today they are a cornerstone of Belgian food culture and a candidate for UNESCO recognition.

🥔 What Makes Them Special

  • Fried twice — once low, once hot
  • Crispy outside, fluffy inside
  • Traditionally fried in beef fat (blanc de bœuf)
  • Cut thicker than French fries

🏠 The Frietkot

Served from a frietkot or frituur — a beloved local fry shack found on nearly every Belgian street corner.

Packed in a paper cone or tray, eaten with a small fork.

🥫 Classic Sauces

Frietjes are rarely eaten plain. The sauce is half the experience:

Mayonnaise Andalouse Samurai Ketchup Curry Joppiesaus Tartare Pickles

💡 Did You Know?

The name "French fries" may come from WWI soldiers who tasted them in French-speaking Belgium — but Belgians insist the fry is theirs. A perfect portion is freshly fried, golden, and salted while still hot. Smakelijk!
