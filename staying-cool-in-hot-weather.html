Staying Cool in Hot Weather

Why it matters — and what to do about it.

Why It Matters

Heat is the deadliest weather hazard. When the body can’t shed heat fast enough, its core temperature climbs — straining the heart, kidneys, and brain. The risk is silent and fast: heat exhaustion can tip into life-threatening heatstroke within minutes.

Staying cool isn’t comfort. It’s protection.

Know the Warning Signs

Heat Exhaustion Heatstroke — emergency
Heavy sweatingHot, dry skin or heavy sweating
Cool, clammy skinConfusion, slurred speech
Dizziness, weaknessBody temp 40°C / 104°F+
Nausea, headacheFainting, seizures

Heatstroke is a medical emergency — call for help immediately.

Stay Cool, Stay Safe

  • Hydrate — drink water before you feel thirsty; skip alcohol and sugary drinks.
  • Seek shade & AC — stay indoors during the hottest hours (11am–4pm).
  • Dress light — loose, pale, breathable clothing; a hat outdoors.
  • Slow down — postpone hard activity to early morning or evening.
  • Cool the body — cool showers, damp cloths, fans, feet in cool water.
  • Never leave children or pets in a parked car — ever.

Check on Others

The most vulnerable are the elderly, the very young, people who are ill or isolated, and outdoor workers. A two-minute phone call or visit can save a life.

Cool body. Clear mind. Stay safe.
